Phase portrait

Each point is one moment: prey along the bottom, predators up the side. The path loops as the two populations chase each other.

path   where one group stops changing   balance point

Where the dashed lines cross, both populations hold steady. With no food limit, the path circles that point forever. With a food limit, it spirals in and settles.

Try this

  1. Watch the lag. Use Cycles. Predators peak after prey, because they need time to grow on a full food supply.
  2. Add a food limit. Press Limited food. The swings shrink and both groups settle at the balance point.
  3. Make predators better hunters. Raise Predation rate. The cycles get bigger, and the prey balance does not change.
  4. Cause a collapse. Press Collapse. Prey crash so far that fewer than one animal is left, and both groups die out.

The biology

Prey grow at a steady rate until predators eat them. Predators turn what they eat into offspring and die at a steady rate. This is the Lotka–Volterra model, first written down in the 1920s.

The model is simplified: one prey, one predator, an even habitat, and smooth numbers. Real populations also face disease, weather, hiding places and many other species.
