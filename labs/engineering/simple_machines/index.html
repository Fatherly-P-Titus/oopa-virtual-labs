Ideal MA ignores losses. Actual effort uses efficiency (and friction on the plane when μ > 0).

Try this

  1. Leverage. On a class 1 lever, lengthen the effort arm. Ideal MA rises and the effort falls.
  2. Class 3. Switch to class 3: the effort arm is shorter than the load arm, so MA < 1 — useful for speed or range, not force.
  3. Steep ramp. On the inclined plane, raise the angle. Effort climbs; add friction and it climbs further.
  4. More strands. On the pulley, increase supporting strands. Ideal MA equals that number.

The engineering

A simple machine trades force against distance. Mechanical advantage is load divided by effort. In the ideal case there is no friction, so work in equals work out.

Real machines lose energy to friction and deformation. Efficiency \(\eta\) is actual MA divided by ideal MA, so the effort you must apply is \(E = W / (MA_{\mathrm{ideal}} \cdot \eta)\).
