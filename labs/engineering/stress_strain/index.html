σ = F/A, ε = δ/L, δ = FL/(AE), n = σy/σ. Linear elastic idealisation up to yield.

Try this

  1. Stress vs area. Halve the diameter. Area falls by four; stress rises by four.
  2. Stiffer material. Switch steel → rubber. Elongation jumps; E is much smaller.
  3. Safety factor. Raise the force until n drops below 1 — the model flags yield.
  4. Length. Longer bars stretch more for the same stress (same ε, larger δ).

The engineering

Stress is force per unit area; strain is elongation per unit length. In the linear elastic range they are linked by Young’s modulus. Design often keeps working stress well below yield so the safety factor stays comfortably above one.

This lab is uniaxial and uniform. Real parts have stress concentrations, multiaxial states and nonlinear material curves that need more advanced analysis.
