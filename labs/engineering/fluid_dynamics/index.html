Try this

  1. Venturi. Narrow the throat. Speed rises, pressure falls — the classic effect used in flow meters.
  2. Continuity. Check that v₂/v₁ ≈ (D₁/D₂)².
  3. Friction. Raise f and watch total head H₂ drop below H₁.
  4. Elevation. Lift the throat (z₂). Gravitational head trades with pressure.

The engineering

For steady incompressible flow, volume flow rate is constant along the pipe. Where the section shrinks, speed must rise. Bernoulli then says pressure usually falls — unless height or losses change the balance.

Real pipes also need a friction model (Moody chart, fittings). This lab keeps a single factor so the ideal Bernoulli structure stays clear.
