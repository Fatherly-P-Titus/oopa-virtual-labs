Try this

  1. Reduction. On a simple pair, set N₂ twice N₁. Output speed halves; torque roughly doubles.
  2. Idler direction. Switch to Idler. Input and output rotate the same way; the middle gear only passes motion.
  3. Compound reduction. On Compound, increase N₂ and N₄. The overall ratio multiplies the two stages.
  4. Efficiency. Lower efficiency and watch output torque fall while speeds stay the same.

The engineering

Meshing spur gears have a speed ratio equal to the inverse tooth ratio, and they turn in opposite directions. An idler adds a second reversal so the output matches the input sense without changing the overall ratio magnitude (still set by first and last gears).

A compound shaft multiplies stage ratios, giving large reductions in a compact layout. Ideal torque scales with the inverse of the speed ratio; real trains lose a fraction of power at each mesh.
