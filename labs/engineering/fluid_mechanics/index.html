Try this

  1. Depth. In Hydrostatics, double h — gauge pressure doubles.
  2. Mercury. Switch to mercury: the same depth gives a much larger pressure.
  3. Manometer. Change Δh and watch Δp. Mercury needs a smaller column for the same Δp.
  4. Float or sink. In Buoyancy, raise object density above the fluid density — it sinks and F_B < W.

The engineering

In a static fluid, pressure depends only on depth and density. Manometers turn a pressure difference into a readable height. Archimedes’ principle says the buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced — the key to floating structures and ships.

This lab is hydrostatics only. Flowing fluids (Bernoulli, head loss) are covered in the separate Fluid Dynamics project.
