Try this

  1. Helium. Almost ideal — red and blue isotherms nearly overlap.
  2. CO₂ dense. Low T, small V: attractions dominate, P_vdw < P_ideal, Z < 1.
  3. High pressure. Shrink V until excluded volume (b) pushes P_vdw above ideal.
  4. Critical. Load the critical-point preset and inspect the flat inflection on the red curve.
  5. Custom a, b. Raise a alone — pressure drops. Raise b alone — pressure rises at small V.

The chemistry

Ideal gases ignore molecular size and attractions. van der Waals adds a volume correction \(nb\) and an attractive term \(a n^2/V^2\). At moderate densities attractions lower pressure (Z < 1); at very high densities the finite volume raises pressure (Z > 1).

The critical constants follow from \(a\) and \(b\). Real fluids need better equations (Peng–Robinson, etc.), but van der Waals still teaches the essential physics.
