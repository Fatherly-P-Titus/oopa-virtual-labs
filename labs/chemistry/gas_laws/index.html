Pressure is calculated from PV = nRT. The dots are for illustration: they do not collide with each other.

Try this

  1. Squeeze it. In Boyle’s law, halve the volume. The pressure doubles, and P × V in the table stays the same.
  2. Heat it. In Charles’s law, raise the temperature and the piston rises. Look at the graph: the line points back to 0 K.
  3. Lock it. In Gay-Lussac’s law the volume cannot change, so heating the gas raises its pressure. This is why sealed cans are dangerous in a fire.
  4. Add gas. In Avogadro’s law, add more gas at constant pressure. The volume grows in proportion.

The physics

All four laws are special cases of the ideal gas law, PV = nRT, with R = 8.314 J/(mol·K). With P in kPa and V in litres, PV is in joules.

Temperature must be in kelvin: it starts at absolute zero, where an ideal gas would have no pressure or volume left. Hotter gas means faster particles, which hit the walls harder and more often.
