Try this

  1. Daniell. Zn–Cu at 1 M: E° ≈ 1.10 V. Electrons flow Zn → Cu in the wire.
  2. Nernst. Lower [Cu²⁺] or raise [Zn²⁺] — E falls as Q rises.
  3. Concentration cell. Load the preset: same metal, different concentrations. E is small but non-zero.
  4. SHE. Zn vs hydrogen electrode: the voltmeter reads the standard potential of zinc (magnitude).
  5. Swap. Swap sides — anode/cathode labels follow E°, not left/right position.

The chemistry

In a galvanic cell, spontaneous redox drives electron flow through an external circuit. Reduction occurs at the cathode (higher reduction potential); oxidation at the anode. The salt bridge maintains charge balance with ion flow.

The Nernst equation corrects E° for non-standard concentrations. ΔG = −nFE links the cell voltage to chemical free energy; a positive E means a spontaneous cell (ΔG < 0). Equilibrium constant K follows from E° alone.
