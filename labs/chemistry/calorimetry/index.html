Adiabatic ideal calorimeter. c_water = 4.184 J·g⁻¹·°C⁻¹. Sign: q_rxn < 0 for exothermic processes.

Try this

  1. Copper. Load Cu metal. Note T_f and that q_metal ≈ −q_water.
  2. Aluminium. Al has a higher c — more heat stored for the same mass and ΔT.
  3. Cold pack. NH₄NO₃ dissolves endothermically; the cup cools.
  4. Neutralisation. Equal moles of strong acid and base; ΔH per mole of water.
  5. Bomb. Raise sample mass — ΔT scales; ΔU per mole stays the same.

The chemistry

Calorimetry converts a temperature change into an energy change. In a coffee-cup (constant pressure) experiment, q_p ≈ ΔH for the process in the cup. In a bomb (constant volume), q_v ≈ ΔU; enthalpy follows with a small gas-mole correction.

This lab is adiabatic and instantaneous. Real data need cooling-curve corrections and careful heat-capacity calibrations — the ideal balance still teaches the core accounting.
