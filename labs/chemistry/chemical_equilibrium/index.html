Ideal concentrations. Equilibrium is solved instantly (no kinetics). Kc shifts stand in for temperature via van’t Hoff.

Try this

  1. Kc. Raise Kc — products grow. Lower it — reactants dominate.
  2. Add product. Spike the product amount; the system shifts left and consumes some of it.
  3. Haber + compress. Switch to Haber and halve the volume — equilibrium moves toward NH₃ (fewer gas moles).
  4. ICE table. Read I → C → E for every species after each change.

The chemistry

At equilibrium the reaction quotient Q equals Kc. The ICE table tracks how far the system moves from the initial state. Le Chatelier’s principle predicts the direction of the shift when concentration, volume or temperature (here via Kc) changes.

This lab solves the mass-action equation directly. Real systems also have finite rates; kinetics are covered in Reaction Rates.
