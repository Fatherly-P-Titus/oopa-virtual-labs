Challenge

The strength of the flask solution is hidden. Titrate to the colour change, record the endpoint, then calculate:
flask strength = burette strength × volume added ÷ flask volume

Solutions are at 25 °C. The burette holds 50 mL.

Try this

  1. Find the jump. Strong acid with strong base. Add 1 mL at a time. Near 25 mL the pH leaps from about 3 to about 11.
  2. Go slowly. Switch to single drops as the colour begins to change. One drop can tip the whole flask.
  3. Pick the wrong indicator. Weak acid with strong base, then choose methyl orange. It changes colour long before the equivalence point.
  4. Be the analyst. Tick Unknown flask solution, find the endpoint, and calculate the strength.

The chemistry

In a titration a solution of known strength (the titrant) is added to a measured volume of another until they have exactly reacted. This is the equivalence point. Here, acid and base react 1 : 1, so CaVa = CbVb at that point.

An indicator changes colour over a narrow pH range, which marks the endpoint. A weak acid or base gives a smaller pH jump, and the equivalence point is not at pH 7, so the indicator must be chosen to match.
